Particles and Matter Lab

Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. All matter is made of tiny particles, far too small to see, even with a microscope. The particles are always moving.

How small is a particle?

If an ice cube was made as big as the Earth, each of its particles would be about the size of a grape. Particles are so small that one drop of water contains more than a thousand million million million of them.

Heating gives particles more energy. At the melting point they break free of their fixed places; at the boiling point they escape from each other completely. While a substance changes state, its temperature stays the same. Changes of state are reversible, and the mass stays the same because no particles are made or lost.

Diffusion is the spreading out of particles from where there are lots of them to where there are fewer. It happens because particles move about randomly. It is faster in gases than in liquids, and faster when it is hotter.

A pure substance contains only one substance. A mixture contains two or more substances that are not joined together. When a solute dissolves in a solvent it makes a solution. The solute particles spread out between the solvent particles: they do not disappear, so the mass stays the same.

Key words

Solute: the substance that dissolves. Solvent: the liquid it dissolves in. Solution: the mixture they make. Soluble: it can dissolve. Insoluble: it cannot dissolve. Saturated: no more solute will dissolve.
